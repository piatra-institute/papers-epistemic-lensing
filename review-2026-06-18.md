# Review: epistemic-lensing

## Verdict

**Reject** (as submitted; the conceptual core is salvageable for a different, non-empirical venue). The paper is a clean, often vivid conceptual framework, but its central empirical claim is unbacked: Section 5 presents a "toy simulation" with specific numerical results (information loss ≈ 0.70/0.55/0.05/0.35, posterior divergence ≈ 0.08/0.31/0.19/0.24, hysteresis ≈ 0.12/0.21) as if computed, yet **no code in the repository implements that model, there is no results file, and the author's own `audit.md` records the numbers as "illustrative ≈ values, claims_target=none."** The single most serious problem: the prose reports invented illustrative numbers in the present tense ("Results," "the toy model demonstrates") when nothing was simulated — at a rigorous venue that is disqualifying until the model is actually built and run.

## Argument

- **§5 (the load-bearing weakness):** the empirical section is fictional. The gap: results are reported in the present tense as accomplished when no model was run. Everything Section 5.4 and the conclusion lean on ("two failure modes… ignorance and distortion are qualitatively different") rests on numbers that were typed, not measured.
- **§5.2 vs §5.3:** "We run the model under four channel configurations, each isolating a different distortion operator" — but **Scenario B is "Selection + warping,"** two operators at once, and there is no pure-selection and no pure-warping run. The high divergence of B is then attributed to "warping" in §5.3/§5.4. The scenarios neither isolate cleanly nor cover the five-operator taxonomy.
- **§4.3 (technical error in the "deflection" criterion):** with $\kappa = u_\text{mediated} - u_\text{benchmark}$, the stated condition for wrong-direction updating, "$\text{sign}(\kappa) \neq \text{sign}(u_\text{benchmark})$," is incorrect. If $u_\text{benchmark}=+2$ and $u_\text{mediated}=+1$ (same direction, merely attenuated), then $\kappa=-1$ and $\text{sign}(\kappa)\neq\text{sign}(u_\text{benchmark})$ — falsely flagged as deflection. The condition should be on $\text{sign}(u_\text{mediated})$, not $\text{sign}(\kappa)$.
- **§4.3 / §3 (geometry as decoration):** "inferential curvature," "geodesics," "the geometry of the inferential surface," "second-order property" — but $\kappa$ as defined is a *first difference of first derivatives*, not a curvature in any geometric sense. No manifold, metric, or second derivative is ever defined. The differential-geometry vocabulary is metaphor presented as formalism.
- **§2.2 (the Markov blanket is non-load-bearing):** the blanket formalism is invoked in the title and §2.2 but does nothing in the model or metrics — the §5 model is a scalar filter with a channel transform that needs no blanket partition. The "Markov-blanket account" is relabeling, not machinery.
- **§6 (population phenomena are coinage without mechanism):** posterior shear, focal capture, shadow zones, narrative gravity wells are each one paragraph of definition with no model, no simulation, and no data. The shear/polarization distinction (§6.2) is genuinely sharp; the rest reads as naming for naming's sake.
- **Genuinely strong:** the ignorance-vs-distortion thesis is a real, crisp contribution; the five-operator taxonomy maps intuitively and usefully onto real media mechanisms; §8's caveats are honest and self-aware (it concedes the benchmark is approximate and the toy model is illustrative); the Benkler engagement on asymmetry is the right move.

The two "simulations" the paper gestures at (the Gaussian toy model described in §5; the LLM persona experiment in PROTOCOL.md) operationalize the same-named metrics differently, and `NarrativeNavigator.py` implements neither. Not a factual error in the prose per se, but the empirical apparatus is internally incoherent.
- No erroneous dates, venues, or technical definitions found beyond the above (the Shannon, Kahneman/Tversky, Pearl, Friston, Pariser, Sunstein details are correct).

## Prose and insight

**Alive:** the opening (§1) is the best writing in the paper — "They inhabit *different inferential geometries*," "the real question is not volume of information but *shape of transformation*," and the sustained optical conceit (a lens bends light, it does not block it). The ignorance/distortion framing in §9 lands. The §8 caveats are unusually honest for this genre.

**Dead:** §7 (Empirical Program) is a competent, forgettable variable list ("Source concentration: the Herfindahl index…") that any methods section could contain. §6's named phenomena are evocative labels stretched over thin definitions; "narrative gravity wells" and "posterior shear" are coinages doing rhetorical rather than analytical work. The cumulative effect is a **vocabulary-generation exercise** — five operators, four metrics, three curvature regimes, four population phenomena — long on naming, short on anything measured.

**Genuine contribution:** the distinction between *deficit of signal* and *deformation of the inferential path*, with the claim that they have different dynamics and different remedies, is real and worth stating. The operator taxonomy is a usable analytic vocabulary. But the paper as written promises a *measurable, simulated* science of distortion and delivers neither the measurements nor the simulation.
